Cicero wrote that he had seen Archimedes’ tomb. There were no inscriptions, or they had been erased — there was a war, and the place passed from hand to hand. Cicero recognized the monument from a description: a hemisphere, a cylinder, and a cone were engraved in the marble. Archimedes was proud of this theorem of his and bequeathed that it be carved on his tombstone.

Interestingly, the proof is within reach of a schoolchild who has learned the area of a circle and the Pythagorean theorem.
