Salman Khan — founder of Khan Academy — gave Marina Mogilko an interview yesterday, and today I sent Marina a letter about that interview, asking her to pass it on to Khan.

Here is Khan’s problem
1) the list of professions is disappearing, while a list of new ones appears
2) people are ready to retrain and teachers are ready to retrain them, BUT
3) whom to teach for what? clearly, mistakes are costly
4) Khan’s solution: build a park of simulations and run candidates through that park
5) One positive effect we get at once is clear — jobs creating simulations. And that plus is quite enough. But a second plus would also be welcome: that the simulation park, per dollar spent, deliver the effect of the right change of workplace.
6) Khan means to copy on a simulator the environment in which a profession exists. A pilot will be shown blue sky, a milkmaid — pedigree cows. And that, of course, is more orders for simulation makers.
7) But maybe it is time to stop and look at what exactly we are simulating? Outer likeness, or inner structure?
8) Readers already know what structures I found in activity — once you have modeled a structure, you can assemble more and more new simulations from new components.
9) Outer resemblance does not give that; there you need countless simulations, all from scratch. Inner structure, on the contrary, allows it. But for that — read the theory, test it in practice, and put it into the economy.
