I spent some time inside an election campaign for the Israeli parliament (the Knesset). Here is what I understood. Formalized knowledge describes only a small part of what is going on. Already the last parameter we introduced — “Context” — allows only limited formalization. But the way people interact in politics is finer and harder than an average chance communication. I see at least two groups of parameters that I do not know how to formalize.

1) One person’s trust in another.
2) The experience of personal and group relations.

I learned a great deal about how texts are composed and selected, and I learned to analyze these texts. But I understood that practical immersion supplies the participants with such unwritten knowledge that the discoveries of theorists do not surprise practitioners — they already know all of this from experience.

Theory is good, but here it cannot get ahead of practice.

Here is one of those “catch-up thoughts.”

The voter in a society of “universal, unconditional, and equal suffrage” is a person with simple thinking. An experienced leader, in order to attract this mass, speaks simply. But no thought hangs in the air: it is part of a picture of the world — in our case, part of Simplified Picture 1. The leader himself, when governing the country, must use the complex Picture of the World 2.

To hold two pictures and mix nothing up is costly. It is level 8. After several years of successful government a temptation arises: use only picture 1, and hide picture 2 and forget it. (level 4, not 6, and certainly not 7)

Millions of flies can’t be wrong.

And right there — because of the details that were missed — a great catastrophe awaits the system.

But historians already know that a great catastrophe awaits a leader who talks to the crowd and not to the experts.

And here is another one.

A person comes to vote for leader A. Another comes for leader B.
Each list receives 10 seats. But if the lists are merged, the result is not 20 = 10 + 10, but only 15 seats from the voters. Why? Because the level-1 voter got lost and refused to take part.

A joke to follow.
A voter for Bibi cannot vote for Benjamin Netanyahu — for him the name is too long.

Afterword
The experience of personal interactions is like the experience of joint social actions — with one difference: it is hidden from outside eyes. That is, it is no harder than context — but it is not public, and this has to be taken into account.
Many communities have secret rituals and signs that are described nowhere but are passed from hand to hand, and so on. The scale of publicity — from 0 to 1 — has an infinite number of gradations here.
