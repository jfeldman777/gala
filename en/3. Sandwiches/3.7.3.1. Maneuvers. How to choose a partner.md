My prospective partner and I each have four available levels. To compare our profiles, I play eight games: each person starts one game from each of their four levels. I record the moves and outcome of each game. When it ends in cooperation, I record who is in the senior position. The two preceding pages give the rules and examples. You can try your own profiles in the [Maneuvers game](https://jfeldman777.github.io/man/?lang=en).

## Three frames of choice

**There are several candidates.** If there is at least one candidate with no wars, I rule out everyone with whom even one game ended in war. For the remaining candidates, I compare the counts of cooperation in the senior and junior positions separately. If there are wars with everyone, I keep the candidates with the fewest wars. If they tie, I can decide based on other circumstances or choose at random. Then I look at the moves that lead to wars with the partner I chose.

**There is one candidate, and I can decline.** Even one war in eight games is grounds for declining.

**The partner is given, and I cannot decline.** I look at the sequence of moves that leads to war and see which moves I can avoid to prevent it.

## What I may have to hold back

Suppose I can avoid war with one partner by refraining from acting at level 7, and with another by refraining from acting at level 4. The two candidates may have the same number of wars, while the restrictions are different. I still have the ability, but I hold it back in this relationship. If using it matters to me, that creates tension. When I have a choice, I can decide which restriction is easier to live with.
