Imagine that a player of a certain level X is dropped into a territory inhabited by characters of levels from 1 to X−1. (In the simple versions this is labeled; in the hard ones you can figure it out after playing the simple ones.)

Everything happens on a checkered board. The player and the characters move on the cells of this board. The player — by a human’s will; a character — by the formula built into it.

Now the phrase “come next to” is clear: it means “step onto a cell neighboring the given one.”

If the player comes next to a character of level X−1, they take that character into the team. But if they alone approach a character below level X−1, they die (war).

If the player has already gathered a team — for example of levels X and X−1 — they can then join a character of level X−2, and so on.

Goals: 1) do not die 2) gather the largest possible team 3) bring it home

The difficulty of the game illustrates the problem of surviving among those who are much simpler than you = lower in level (read the theory).

Extra task — characters move according to their levels: try to describe the movement type of each level and say how that type of movement relates to the well-known general properties of characters of that level. (Here you need to read the theory.)

[Check whether you know the levels — “Kolobok and his team”](https://jfeldman777.github.io/kolobok/kolobok_2026.html?lang=en)

The game shows one of the strategies of “How a smart person survives”: **do not enter direct interaction with a person whose Level is more than one below yours, but build a chain of intermediaries of neighboring Levels.**

For example, instead of the dangerous contact **5 → 2**, build the chain **5 → 4 → 3 → 2**.

There are two more strategies:

**1. Run**, if a safe interaction cannot be built.

**2. To an address from Level X reply together with X−1 and X+1**, drawing the interlocutor into cooperation with an opaque senior. (This works in live dialogue; it is not implemented in the game.)
